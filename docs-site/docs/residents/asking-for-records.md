# Asking the town for records

Anyone can ask to see or get copies of the public records the town keeps, such as the file on a property or the minutes of a meeting. You do not need an account. On the town's home page, choose **Ask for town records**.

## Fill in the form

The page is titled **Ask for town records** and has four boxes.

- **What records are you asking for?** Required. Say which property, which dates and what kind of record, as far as you know. The box takes up to 5,000 characters.
- **Your name (optional)**.
- **Your email.** Required. The town sends your receipt and its letters to this address, and may send the records there too.
- **Your phone (optional)**.

Only town staff see your name and contact details. Choose **Send request**.

If the form says "Too many requests were sent from here. Wait an hour and try again", the town limits how many requests one address or one connection can send in an hour. Wait and send it again.

## What you get right away

The page that follows is titled **Request received**. It shows your request number, which looks like RK-2026-0001, and the date the town will answer by. Chiban also emails you a receipt with the same number and date. If the receipt email could not be sent, the page says so. Keep the number if you need to ask about your request.

The town has five business days from the day it received the request. Weekends and the town's holidays do not count. Within that time it will make the records available, deny the request in writing, or tell you in writing when it will answer.

## How the town answers

Staff see your request in their list marked **Online**, with the same deadline as a request made at the counter.

- The town's letter about your request is emailed to you as a PDF.
- If staff release the records by email, you get the files attached to the email. When the files are large, you get a link instead.
- A link opens a page titled "Records for request" and your number, where you open each file. The link works for 30 days. See [looking up parcels, meetings and released records](./looking-things-up.md) for what that page shows. If it has expired, call the land use office and ask for a new one.
- Records can also be shown at the town office or sent on paper. In those cases the letter still comes by email.

If the town denies the request, its letter names each record it withheld and the exemption that applies.

If your question is about a permit application you filed, you can also [follow it in your account](./following-your-application.md). To report building or land use work that may break the rules, use [Reporting a possible violation](./reporting-a-problem.md) instead.
