# New Hampshire deadlines and holidays

State law gives the town, its boards and applicants fixed amounts of time to act: 30 days to decide a building permit, 65 days for the Planning Board to decide an accepted application, 5 business days to file a board decision. Chiban calls each of these a clock. Use this page to see how long each clock runs, what starts it, how the days are counted and what happens when it runs out.

## How a clock works

A clock starts when the event that starts it is recorded, such as the application being received or the board voting. It then appears on the application with its due date and the days left, and it shows as overdue once the date passes. When the step it waits for is done, the clock is met and stops.

Some clocks can be moved. Under **Adjust this clock** on the application, staff who may extend or waive clocks can record an **Extension** or a **Waiver**, with a reason, where the rule allows one. Each clock below says whether it can be extended. A **Statutory exception** is for a case the law itself excuses, and it needs the legal authority written down. Administrators record those.

A few lengths are set by the town rather than by statute, such as how long a building permit stays valid. These read "unless the town changes it" below, and the town sets them under **Settings**, **Town rule settings**. Some notice periods are minimums: a town may give more notice than the law requires, never less.

## How the days are counted

Chiban counts days the way RSA 21:35 does.

- The day the clock starts is not counted. The last day is counted.
- A deadline in business days skips Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays.
- When a filing window, such as the time to appeal, runs forward from an event and its last day falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, it ends on the next business day instead.
- A filing window that counts back from a date, such as the last day to file before a Planning Board meeting, does not move for weekends or holidays.
- A deadline for the town or a board to act does not move for weekends or holidays.
- A deadline in months ends on the same date that many months later, or on the last day of a shorter month.

Each clock on an application also shows a **Counting** line.

## Legal holidays

Chiban treats the legal holidays in RSA 288:1 as days the office is closed. A holiday that falls on a Sunday is observed on the Monday after it (RSA 288:2). A holiday that falls on a Saturday is not moved. Biennial election day is a holiday in even-numbered years only.

No other day counts as closed. A day the town office shuts for any other reason, such as a storm, still counts toward every deadline.
