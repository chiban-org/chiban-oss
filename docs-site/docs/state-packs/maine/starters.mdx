# Maine starters

A starter is a ready-made application type. Adopting one copies a complete permit into your settings: its form, its fee schedule, its conditions, its inspection checklists, the letters and permit it prints, and the deadlines state law attaches to it. Use this page to decide which starters your town needs before you adopt them, and to see what each one asks of an applicant.

## Before you adopt

Most fee lines are blank. This catalog shows them as "no charge", and the fee schedule marks each one **Set your fee**. They charge nothing until you enter your town's own amount. Fill them in before you take a real application.

The plumbing and subsurface wastewater fees work differently. They start at the state minimum fees in the rules they cite, 10-144 CMR chapter 240 Table A for internal plumbing and chapter 241 Table 4A for wastewater systems. The internal plumbing and wastewater system fees double when staff check "Work started before permit" on the application. The seasonal conversion fee does not. If your town charges more than the minimum, raise the amounts.

Some inspection checklists show "Source: Chiban starting checklist. The town can edit it." under their name. Chiban wrote those as a starting point. Read them and change them to match how your inspectors work.

Adopting publishes the new application type right away, so it is live as soon as you adopt it. Set its fees, form and letters under **Settings** before you tell the public it is there.

A later release of Chiban can improve a starter you have already adopted. **Check adopted starters for updates**, at the foot of the **Starter templates** page, lists the changes. Tick the ones you want, and everything you leave unticked stays as your town has it.

Adopting a starter needs the permission to edit configuration, which administrators have.

## How to adopt a starter

1. Go to **Settings**, then **Starter templates**.
2. Find the starter under **Ready to adopt** and choose **Adopt this starter**.
3. Open **Settings**, **Fee schedules** and set your town's amounts.
4. Read through the form under **Settings**, **Forms** and the letters under **Settings**, **Templates**, and change anything that does not match how your office works.

## How to read the catalog

Each entry gives the type of proceeding, whether a board hears it, and the letters its application numbers start with. It then lists the fees, the documents the applicant provides, the inspections, and the deadlines Chiban tracks.

Fees are written the way you would quote them at the counter, with when each one falls due. A fee "due with notice" is charged when staff issue the abutter list, because that is when the number of abutters is known.

Each document shows when it is needed. A document needed "to file" must be in before the application can be submitted. "When it applies" means the form asks a question first, and only some answers call for the document.

The inspections are the checklists that come with the permit. The deadlines are the state and town clocks that start on applications of this type, and each one is explained on [deadlines and holidays](./deadlines.mdx).

## Who handles plumbing permits

The internal plumbing, subsurface wastewater and seasonal dwelling conversion permits belong to the local plumbing inspector. Only staff with the **Plumbing inspector** role can issue, decide or inspect them, and the plumbing inspector cannot act on any other kind of permit. Give that role to whoever your town has appointed. If the same person is also your code enforcement officer, give them a second role for the rest of their work, such as **Inspector**.
