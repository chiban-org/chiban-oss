# Court, town counsel and penalty exposure

When the owner has been served, the answer period is over and nothing was fixed, the order goes to the court. Chiban records each step, hands town counsel a packet, and shows what the town could be owed.

## Court steps

Under the order, the **Court** line says whether the motion to enforce can be filed. When it cannot, it lists the reasons: service is not complete, an answer period is still running, an answer was received, an appeal stayed the case, or the order has not been filed with the court clerk.

Record each step with **Record a court step**:

- **Filed with the court clerk**. Every required person must be served, with proof, first.
- **Motion to enforce filed**. Allowed once the answer period is over, no answer came in, and five days have passed since the filing with the clerk. If the five days are not up, Chiban names the first day the motion can be filed.
- **Judgment entered**, with a **Judgment result**: **Order affirmed**, **Order modified** or **Order annulled**. It needs the motion first.

Each step takes a **Date**, the **Court**, the **Docket number** and the court document, which you upload first and then choose. The case moves to **In court** after the first filing, and the steps appear as a dated list.

After a judgment, the page shows the date by which the judgment can be appealed.

## Refer the case to town counsel

Land use staff and administrators refer a case. Under **Town counsel**, choose **Refer to counsel** and enter:

- **Counsel**, the attorney's name.
- **Purpose**: **Motion to enforce**, **Injunction**, **Penalty action** or **Other**.
- **Date referred**.

Chiban builds a counsel packet, a PDF filed under **Documents and photos** as **Counsel packet for** the case number, and links it as **Open packet**. It contains the case details, the findings and notices, each order with its service checklist, the proof of service on file, appeals and stays, and the penalty exposure. Send it to counsel the way the town normally sends documents. Chiban does not email it. An administrator can change the cover page under **Settings**, **Templates**.

When counsel reports back, choose **Record an outcome**. **What happened** can be **Motion filed**, **Hearing held**, **Judgment for the town**, **Judgment for the respondent**, **Settled**, **Dismissed**, **Withdrawn** or **Appealed to the superior court**, with the date, court, docket, document and a note. A judgment for the town leads to the fine.

## Penalty exposure

Under **Fines**, a line reads **Estimated statutory exposure for counsel. Nothing is owed unless a court imposes it.** It tells counsel how large the town's claim could be. It is a running estimate and not a bill.

- Before the town has anything to count from, it reads **Accrual has not started: no written notice has been received and no conviction has been recorded.**
- Once a notice is received, or a conviction recorded, whichever is earlier, it reads like **Accrual began** on a date, then the days since and the estimated amount.
- After a court imposes a fine, the line changes to say only the fines the court imposed are owed.

The daily amount comes from the town's rules in the state pack, not from anything you enter. The same figure goes into the counsel packet.

## Record a fine and payments

Only administrators and finance staff can record a fine a court imposed, because it goes on the ledger. Choose **Record a court-imposed fine** and enter **Based on** (the judgment), **Kind**, **Amount in dollars**, **Date imposed**, **Court** and **Docket number**. Chiban asks for a recorded judgment first. It will not record a fine while an appeal stay is in effect.

The fine then shows what was paid and the balance, for example **Paid $200.00. Balance due $75.00.** **Do this now** reads **Collect the** amount **fine balance**. Land use staff and finance staff can **Record a payment**: **Amount in dollars**, **Paid by** (**Cash** or **Check**), **Check number** and **Payer name**. When the balance reaches zero the case is ready to resolve.
