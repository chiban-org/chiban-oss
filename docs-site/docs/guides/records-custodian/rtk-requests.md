# Log and answer a Right-to-Know request

Use this page from the day a request arrives until the first response goes out. Getting records to the requester and closing the request are on [the next page](./rtk-release.md). Land use staff and records custodians can do everything here.

Residents can send a request themselves from [Ask for town records](../../residents/asking-for-records.md) on the town's public site, without an account. It arrives in your list marked **Online**, with its own number and the same five-day clock. The requester gets a receipt email with the number and the date the town will answer by. Staff log everything else: requests that come in at the counter, by mail, by email or by phone.

## Log the request

1. Open **Right-to-Know** in the staff menu and choose **Log a request**.
2. **Date received** starts as today. Change it if the request came in earlier. It cannot be a future date.
3. **How it arrived** offers In person, Mail, Email, Online and Phone.
4. **Requester name** and **Requester email, phone, or address** are optional. Put an email address in **Requester email, phone, or address** if you have one. With exactly one email address on the request, Chiban can email the town's letters and records to the requester. See [The letter is emailed or printed](#the-letter-is-emailed-or-printed).
5. Put the request in the requester's words under **What was asked for**.
6. Choose **Log request**.

Chiban gives the request a number such as RK-2026-0001 and starts the first-response clock.

## Watch the deadline

The first response is due five business days after the request is received. The day it arrives is not counted, and weekends and the legal holidays on the town's holiday list are skipped. [Deadlines and holidays](../../state-packs/new-hampshire/deadlines.mdx) lists the holidays Chiban uses.

The list puts the soonest date first, and requests with no date running come last. A date that has passed is marked **(past due)**. The **Status** filters above the list narrow it to **Overdue**, **Due this week** or **No deadline running**.

Open a request and the sentence under its title says what is waiting and when it is due, for example "Waiting on: Town. Send the first response. First response due November 17, 2026, 6 days left." Under **Due next**, **How it is counted** shows the start date and the counting method.

You can still record a late response. The request then shows "Answered late" with the due date and the answer date.

## Send the first response

Open the request. Under **Do this now**, **Send the first response**, choose one of four responses.

**Release the records now.** Use this when the records are ready. You find each record, approve the version to release and release it, all from this card. The first release records the response and files its letter. The steps are on [the next page](./rtk-release.md).

**Written statement of the time needed.** Use this when you cannot answer in time. Fill in:

- **Date of response**, which starts as today.
- **Date the town will respond by**, on or after the response date.
- **Why more time is needed**.
- **Charge, if any (what it is for)** and **Charge in dollars**. Fill in both or neither.

The request moves to **Waiting for the date the town stated**, and the deadline becomes the date you gave. From there you either release the records or deny the request. A request takes only one written time statement, and the response choices disappear once it is recorded, so give a date you can keep.

**Records are posted on the town website.** Enter the **Web address where the records are posted** and the date. The request becomes **Responded**, ready to close.

**Denied.** This choice stays greyed out until you have recorded a withheld record with its exemption and explanation. See the next section.

## Record what you are withholding

Under **Records withheld or redacted**, open **Record a withholding**.

- **Record is**: **Withheld in full** or **Released with redactions**.
- **Which record**: the record in your own words.
- **Exemption**: the list of exemptions the state pack offers, or **Made confidential by another law**. For that one, name the law under **Law, if made confidential by another law**.
- **How the exemption applies (a sentence or two)**: at least 20 characters.

Choose **Record withholding**. When you release a copy redacted in Chiban, the exemptions drawn on its boxes are recorded here for you. Enter a withholding by hand only for a record you are keeping back entirely, or for a redaction made outside Chiban. See [Redact a document](./redaction.md).

## Deny a request

Once a withholding is on file, choose **Denied** as the first response. After a time statement, use **Or deny the request** and then **Deny the request** on the **Do this now** card. Either way you enter the **Date of the denial**. Chiban files a denial letter naming every withheld record and its exemption, and the request moves to **Responded**.

## The letter is emailed or printed

Each response files a letter as a PDF, and you can open it from **Letters** on the request. The wording comes from the "Response to your Right-to-Know request" template, which an administrator can edit under **Settings**, **Templates**.

What happens next depends on the requester's contact details:

- **Exactly one email address on the request.** Chiban emails the letter to it as a PDF. A note by the button says "The letter will be emailed to" the address. Afterward the message reads "Response recorded and the letter emailed to" the address, and **Letters** shows "Emailed to" the address and the date.
- **No email address, or more than one.** Chiban does not guess. The note by the button says "There is no email address on this request, so print the letter from Letters and send it." Afterward **Letters** shows "No email address. Print this letter and send it."
- **An email that fails.** The response is still recorded and the letter filed. The message says the email could not be sent, and **Letters** shows "The email to" the address "could not be sent. Print this letter and send it."

This covers the first response, a time statement, a posted-online response and a denial. Letters for releases are covered on [the next page](./rtk-release.md).
